How it behaves

Calm, specific, and it never deletes on its own.

Plain words

Junk items carry a short note from the rule that found them: what they are and what happens if they go. “Xcode rebuilds them the next time you build” beats a progress bar and a big red number.

Knows your tools

Xcode, simulators, npm, Yarn, pnpm, Cargo, Go, Gradle, pip, CocoaPods, JetBrains and more.

  • Junk never lists your Ollama or LM Studio models.

Trash first

Everything goes to the Trash and into a history you can put back from.

  • Files changed in the last 7 days are never pre-selected.
  • iCloud, Mail, Photos and Keychains are off-limits.
  • Empty Trash asks first and shows the size.

    Inside the app

    One click to look. You decide what goes.

    Sweep

    One scan, grouped into tiles you can open and read before anything moves.

    Junk

    Caches, logs, build files and installers, each marked Safe or Review.

    Apps

    Remove an app with the leftovers it scattered, and see which apps have updates.

    Space map

    See which folders are actually big, all the way down.

    Clutter

    Duplicate files, matched by a hash of their whole contents, plus big files you haven't opened in a while.

    History

    Every move is logged. Put an item back where it came from, as long as it's still in the Trash.

    Left out on purpose

    What Dustpan doesn't do.

    • Free RAM buttonsmacOS manages memory better than any button.
    • Scary “threats found”A cache isn't a threat. It's a cache.
    • Magic speed-upsDeleting caches frees space. It doesn't make your Mac faster, so Dustpan won't claim it does.
    • An agent that never quitsNo background helper. The menu-bar item and launch at login are off unless you turn them on.
    • Accounts and trackingNo sign-up, no analytics. The only thing that goes online is the app-update check, and only when you open the Updates tab.

    Dustpan 0.1.0

    9.9 MB · macOS 14 Sonoma or later · Apple silicon and Intel

    Download the disk image
    1. Open the disk image and drag Dustpan into Applications.
    2. Open Dustpan. macOS will say it can't check the app for malicious software. That's because Dustpan isn't notarized: it's a free app made without a paid Apple developer account. Close the message.
    3. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down, and click Open Anyway next to Dustpan. You only do this once.
    4. When Dustpan asks, turn on Full Disk Access so it can see your caches, Downloads and Trash. It works without it, but sees less.
    Want to check the file? Its SHA-256 checksum:
